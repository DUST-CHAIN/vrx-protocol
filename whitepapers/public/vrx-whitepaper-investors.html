Executive summary

The thesis, in one page

VRX is a reservation primitive: a callable NEAR smart contract that records who reserved what, paired with a private off-chain portal that carries everything the chain must not — identities, prices, specifications and deal terms. Its market is any business that sells unique, non-substitutable inventory through channels that do not trust one another.

When each item is one-of-a-kind — a specific apartment, a specific pedigree animal, a specific machine — double-booking it across channels is not an inconvenience, it is a breached contract. Yet the tools that could give everyone a shared answer all fail the confidentiality test: a shared database or CRM centralizes trust in one vendor and leaks who is buying what at what price; a naïve public-chain listing leaks the very terms that make a deal confidential, forever. The market has had to choose between shared truth and privacy. VRX refuses the trade-off.

The design answer — and the moat

VRX enforces a strict split. The chain holds only opaque hashes and neutral account ids: it proves that a reservation exists and who holds it, and nothing else. The meaning — the what, the how-much, the with-whom — lives off-chain under server-side authorization and field-level masking. Layered on top is a vetted operator network and an owned forensic/provenance capability. The moat is not a faster database; it is the discipline of what is never written to the ledger at all, plus the human-vetting layer and IP others have not built.

Section 1 · The market problem

Selling the unique, through channels that distrust each other

A whole class of high-value commerce shares one shape: scarce, identified inventory sold by an owner through many intermediaries who compete with one another. The existing toolchain breaks on it in three compounding ways — each with a direct cost.

Failure 1 — double-booking a unique thing

When inventory is fungible (seats, generic SKUs) an oversell is an inconvenience you reconcile later. When each item is unique, two channels reserving "312" at once is a hard conflict: one buyer must be told the thing they were promised is gone. In off-plan property that can mean a lost sale, a damaged agency relationship, and legal exposure on a signed reservation. The market needs one authoritative answer to "is this exact unit still available?" that every channel reads the same way, in real time — and no shared spreadsheet or per-agency portal delivers it across parties who will not share a database.

Failure 2 — privacy leakage

The naïve fixes leak. A shared spreadsheet or a single vendor database exposes who is buying what, at what price, through whom — to the vendor and often to rival channels. Putting the registry on a public blockchain is worse: now prices, parties and terms are world-readable forever. The commercially-sensitive and personal details of a deal are exactly what must not be shared to get the shared truth. For a developer, a leaked price list or buyer book is a competitive and regulatory liability, not a footnote.

Failure 3 — circumvention / disintermediation

A channel that learns the end-buyer and the operator can be tempted to cut the introducer out and deal direct — the commission disappears and the relationship that generated the lead is destroyed. The obvious counter-measure, hiding the buyer's identity until a deal is firm, is itself a privacy problem, and it must be reconciled with the operator's legitimate need to confirm a booking. The money and the trust are on the same seam.

The tension in one line

Everyone needs to agree on the same reservation state, while almost no one is entitled to see the same reservation details. A single store that satisfies the first requirement usually violates the second. VRX exists to hold both at once — and that gap is the market it addresses.

Section 2

What VRX is

A callable NEAR smart contract — the reservation and hold registry — plus an off-chain portal: the role-scoped apps each participant uses. Together they are the reservation primitive.

The primitive, precisely

VRX records reservations over non-substitutable inventory. Its on-chain state is deliberately thin: a unit is open, on hold, closed, or done; a claim is open, accepted, declined, or confirmed; an exclusive window is held by one agency or it is not. Everything that would identify the unit, the buyer, the price or the terms is an opaque hash. The contract does not model "apartment" or "car" — it models availability and holds over keys it cannot read.

Value in this system scales with the worth of the specific reserved item. The primitive's whole reason to exist is that substituting one unit for another is not acceptable — so the registry tracks each unit individually and a hold on one is never a hold on another. This is why the addressable value per transaction is high: these are not low-ticket bookings.

Section 2.5 · Competitive landscape

Why nothing in the market does this

Stated plainly, not boastfully — the moat is a design choice others have not made, not a claim of being uniquely clever. Each existing approach gives you one half of what the problem needs and pays for it with the other half.

Three existing approaches, and where each leaks

ApproachGives youWhat it costs
Generic booking / CRM software A shared calendar and pipeline. Centralizes trust in one vendor's database; the vendor (and often rival channels) can see who buys what at what price. No independently-provable record; the operator still owns the single point of truth and failure.
Naïve on-chain listing A tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record. Publishes prices, parties and terms to the world forever. The confidentiality that makes a deal a deal is destroyed to gain the shared truth.
Point-to-point / off-platform deals Privacy by obscurity. No shared availability, so double-booking and circumvention are endemic; no neutral record when a dispute arises.

VRX's position

VRX takes the tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record from the on-chain approach and the confidentiality from the off-platform approach, and reconciles them with a split: the chain carries proof-of-state over opaque keys; a private, access-controlled off-chain layer carries the meaning. No incumbent in the booking / CRM space is built this way, and no generic "put it on-chain" project solves the confidentiality half. VRX sits in the gap between them on purpose.

Honest limits of the claim

  • The off-chain layer's confidentiality rests on server-side authorization, not cryptography — VRX must be trusted to run it correctly. A standing security/privacy audit is how that trust is made checkable, and it is a submittable artifact to any partner who asks.
  • The on-chain opacity is absolute by construction, but it only protects what lives on-chain; a leak, if it happened, would come from the off-chain layer — which is where almost all the engineering attention goes.
  • VRX is not the only system that splits on/off chain. Its specific, defensible contribution is the reservation-primitive framing, the vetted operator network, and the owned provenance/forensic layer — not the generic idea of anchoring a hash.

Section 2.5 · Competitive landscape

The moat, component by component

A defensible position in this category is not one feature; it is the compounding of four things that are each individually buildable but collectively hard to assemble and harder to copy credibly.

1 — The on/off-chain split as a discipline, not a toggle

The hardest part of the design is restraint: deciding what is never written to the chain. That discipline runs through every surface and every migration of the contract's state schema. A competitor cannot bolt it on later — retrofitting confidentiality onto a system that already leaks terms means rebuilding it. VRX started from this constraint, so the whole architecture is shaped by it.

2 — Human vetting as the trust anchor

Mutually-distrusting parties transact on one shared truth only because a trusted entity vets who enters the network and anchors the fact of each sign-off. That vetting is human-led and operated as a business. It is the part of the system that cannot be replaced by code alone — and it is exactly the part that incumbents in pure-software booking tools do not have.

3 — The owned provenance / forensic capability

Sensitive imagery that must be shown to a counterparty is provenance-stamped so a leaked image is traceable back to the recipient it was served to — while remaining anonymous to whoever holds it. The mechanism is deliberately private; it is the moat, and it is owned IP. In this public document we state only the capability: a leaked sensitive image can be attributed by VRX, which changes the incentive to leak.

4 — The network, once it exists

Like any trust network, VRX gets more valuable as more vetted operators and channels join: each developer brings agencies, each agency brings agents and buyers. The split architecture is what lets rivals coexist on the same network without exposing each other — which is the precondition for the network to form at all.

The compounding

Any one of these is copyable. Together — the split that must be designed in from the start, a vetting business, owned forensic IP, and a network that benefits from the split — they are a position a fast follower cannot assemble by shipping a feature. That is the investable moat.

Section 3 · Architecture

The on-chain / off-chain split

One rule governs the whole system: meaning never touches the chain. The figure shows what lives where, and the one-way binding between them.

Why one-way matters

An anchored seal_hash lets anyone later prove a specific off-chain record is the one that was committed (re-seal it, compare). It never lets anyone recover the record from the hash. Tamper-evidence without disclosure is the whole trick — and it is what makes the shared truth safe to publish.

Section 3 · Architecture

One booking, end to end

Follow a single deal from live availability to a confirmed hold, watching where meaning stays and only a hash moves. The contract is deliberately thin; the product value is in orchestrating this flow safely.

The step sequence

  1. Operator stamps a tile → unit open (opaque unit_key).
  2. Agent opens a claim (or burns a booking token) → an opaque request_hash anchors the off-chain request; the unit moves to hold with a timed expiry.
  3. Off-chain: deposit recorded, buyer profile sealed, masked to the developer by default.
  4. Developer replies / confirms → state advances; a consented reveal of the buyer is a separate ceremony.
  5. On completion the tile is finalized → done; expired holds auto-release back to open, so scarce capacity is never stranded.

Section 4 · Privacy

The privacy model — the moat as capability

Two distinct protections, applied per surface: on-chain opacity by construction, and off-chain access control by the server. This is the capability that converts a public chain into infrastructure for confidential commerce.

Protection 1 — on-chain opacity (by construction)

Everything on the contract is an opaque hash (unit, request, seal, commission) plus neutral account ids and status codes. A chain scraper sees "account X holds an exclusive on hash 07f2…" — state, never meaning. The building, unit, price, party and terms cannot be derived from the chain. This protection holds by design and needs no further work; it is the half of the moat that is mathematically done.

Protection 2 — off-chain access control (by the server)

The plaintext that carries meaning lives off-chain, protected by server-side authorization + field-level masking, not by cryptography. The engineering question for every counterparty-facing surface is: is this read scoped to a caller entitled to it, and masked server-side before the wire? A UI blur is theatre; masking happens on the server, so the plaintext never reaches the browser for a masked field. This is where VRX concentrates its engineering, and where the standing audit focuses.

Salted seals give tamper-evidence without reversibility; identity-document images are encrypted at rest; the dispute chat is append-only and hash-chained so a back-dated edit is detectable. Section 5 · Network & business model

Non-circumvention & booking tokens

Two mechanisms keep the network honest and give it an economic rhythm: the buyer stays masked until a deal is firm (so a channel cannot be cut out), and reservation capacity is a scarce, tamper-evident token that cascades down the trust chain.

Non-circumvention

Trust is enforced by visibility, not disclosure. An agent holding a booking has the unit off-market; the developer and agency see who holds it but not necessarily the private buyer behind it until a consented, sealed reveal. A non-circumvention declaration is sealed as evidence at the reveal request — even if the reveal is later declined — so the introduction is on the tamper-evident trail. This is the mechanism that protects an introducer's commission, which is precisely what gets channels to participate.

The booking-token cascade

VRX issues a developer a scarce quantity of booking tokens — reservation capacity. These are handed or sold down the trust chain (developer → agency → agent), with a tamper-evident receipt at each hand-off. An agent holding a token can secure a booking and take a deposit. Capacity is scarce and reusable: a released or cancelled token returns up the chain. Tokens surface as "coins" in the wallet.

Why scarcity is a design choice, not a sales gimmick

Making reservation capacity scarce and reusable is what makes a hold mean something: it cannot be created freely, so holding a unit off-market carries a real cost, and releasing it returns capacity to the network. On-chain this is backed by a non-transferable booking token with burn + rebirth, and the exclusive-hold primitive grants one agency the sole right to book a unit for a bounded window that auto-reverts to the shared pool at expiry.

Section 6 · The economic layer

$VRX — the in-system credit

The economic layer has three objects: the $VRX credit ledger, booking tokens as reservation capacity (above), and commission agreements with a settlement projection. All are privacy-preserving; none puts money terms on-chain.

Read this first — what $VRX is, and is not

$VRX is an in-system credit ledger used to meter and settle activity inside the VRX network. It is not a listed or tradeable coin in VRX's story, not a token sale, not a liquid asset, and it is not redeemable for cash. Supply is fixed and treasury-held. Today it is an off-chain ledger; an on-chain NEP-141 fungible-token form is planned but does not change what it is. Nothing in this document should be read as an offer of, or a market-value claim about, a token.

Why a credit at all

A fixed, treasury-held credit gives the network a neutral internal unit to account for usage and settle between participants without each pair needing a bilateral billing relationship. Because it is fixed and non-inflationary, the accounting stays honest: credit moves between accounts, it is not conjured. Keeping it off-chain today is deliberate — the credit model is chain-agnostic, so the on-chain form can be added later without reworking how the network accounts for value.

Booking tokens are distinct from $VRX

A booking token is a unit of capacity to reserve, not a credit. It is scarce, cascades down the trust chain, and returns on release (above). Keeping capacity (tokens) and accounting (credit) as separate objects is what lets the network price reservation scarcity independently of how it settles money.

Section 6 · The economic layer

Commissions, settlement & the revenue model

Section 8 · Legal posture & contact

Legal posture, and how to reach us

The posture is structural and deliberately narrow: VRX facilitates and seals; it does not adjudicate and does not warrant enforceability. That narrowness is a feature for an investor, not a gap.

What VRX is, legally

  • A facilitator and a sealer. VRX provides the private shared truth and the tamper-evident record. It records that a reservation, agreement or change happened, and seals it.
  • Not an adjudicator. VRX does not decide disputes or determine the outcome of a disagreement between parties. It records the evidence (before/after, who was notified, the sealed declarations) — never the dispute's resolution.
  • Not a warrantor of enforceability. VRX does not warrant that any underlying contract is legally enforceable. Parties' own terms and local law govern.
  • Privacy-by-design. The architecture itself (opaque on-chain, masked off-chain) is the primary privacy control, not a policy promise layered on afterwards.

Why the narrowness is a feature

By refusing to adjudicate or warrant, VRX stays a neutral infrastructure provider across jurisdictions and asset classes. The sealed, append-only record it produces is designed to be useful evidence to the parties and their own counsel — not a substitute for them. This is what keeps the primitive domain-agnostic and multi-jurisdiction-ready (multi-jurisdiction packs are planned), and it is what keeps VRX out of the regulatory category it would otherwise fall into by moving money or ruling on disputes.

The oversight view

The Authority-Review role is the legal posture made concrete: an oversight party can confirm the vetted state of the system without ever seeing private buyer data. Oversight of process, not exposure of parties — the moat extends even to the regulator view.
