For the judges, in one line

A public ledger of reservations that leaks none of the deal. The chain proves that a reservation exists and who holds it; the meaning stays confidential off-chain. It is built and running — testnet contract live, hosted demo reachable, one-command walk-through.

Abstract

In one page

VRX is a reservation primitive: a callable NEAR smart contract that records who reserved what, paired with an off-chain portal that carries everything the chain must not — identities, prices, specifications and deal terms.

An operator with unique, non-substitutable inventory (unit 312 is not unit 311) needs to feed live availability to many sales teams and agencies who do not trust one another, and let them browse, book, reserve, take a deposit and hold — without double-booking, without leaking the commercial or personal details of a deal, and without a channel being able to go around the operator. Generic booking software centralizes trust in one vendor's database; a naïve on-chain listing leaks the very terms that make a deal confidential. VRX reconciles both.

The design answer is a strict split. The chain holds only opaque hashes and neutral account ids: it proves that a reservation exists and who holds it, and nothing else. The meaning — the what, the how-much, the with-whom — lives off-chain under server-side authorization and field-level masking. Privacy is therefore not a promise bolted on; it is a property of what is and is not written to the ledger.

The shape of the claim

Real estate (off-plan property) is the beachhead and the working proof. But the contract never "knows" it is real estate — every real-world reference is an opaque hash — so the same primitive serves cars, livestock, equipment, event slots: any Class-A, non-substitutable inventory.

Why it wins

Three tracks, one build

VRX is not three demos wearing a trench coat. It is a single system whose core property — proof on-chain, meaning off-chain — lands squarely in three judging tracks at once.

Open Innovation

VRX is a genuinely new on-chain primitive, not a fork of an existing pattern: a reservation registry for non-substitutable inventory, shared across mutually-distrusting channels. The contract is domain-agnostic by construction (every real-world reference is an opaque hash), so the same deployment serves off-plan property today and cars, livestock, equipment or event slots unchanged. The contract is real — Rust, near-sdk 5.26.1, seven borsh-safe state migrations, live on testnet — not a slide.

Privacy & Private AI

Privacy is the moat, not a feature. No personal data, no prices, no deal terms ever go on-chain — only opaque hashes and neutral account ids. The confidentiality that makes a deal a deal is preserved while the shared truth is public. Sensitive imagery is served through a two-stage reveal and is provenance-stamped so a leaked image is traceable — and the stamping mechanism is deliberately private (the moat). An outsider's own AI can evaluate fit off-platform from a published machine-readable guide, without being handed private data.

Real-World Money

VRX moves real commerce: live availability, deposits taken against a timed hold, commission agreements anchored on-chain (percentages and splits stay off-chain), a two-sided payment-attestation ledger, and an in-system $VRX credit (claim / send / spend). Booking capacity is itself a scarce, tamper-evident token that cascades down the trust chain. On-rail settlement and the on-chain NEP-141 form of $VRX are planned — stated honestly, not implied as shipped.

The through-line

Each track is satisfied by the same architectural choice — the on-chain / off-chain split — so the privacy claim, the innovation claim and the money claim all rest on one proven mechanism rather than three separate bolt-ons.

The problem

Why nothing already does this

Selling scarce, one-of-a-kind inventory through many channels that do not trust each other breaks the tools everyone already uses — in three ways that compound.

  • Double-booking a unique thing. When each item is unique — a specific apartment, a specific pedigree animal, a specific machine — two channels reserving "312" at once is a hard conflict. The market needs one authoritative answer to "is this exact unit still available?" that every channel reads the same way, in real time.
  • Privacy leakage. A shared spreadsheet or single vendor database exposes who is buying what, at what price, through whom. Putting the registry on a public chain naïvely is worse — prices, parties and terms become world-readable forever.
  • Circumvention. A channel that learns the end-buyer and the operator can be tempted to cut the introducer out and deal direct. Hiding the buyer until a deal is firm is itself a privacy problem that must be reconciled with the operator's need to confirm a booking.

Three existing approaches, and where each leaks

ApproachGives youWhat it costs
Generic booking / CRM software A shared calendar and pipeline. Centralizes trust in one vendor's database; the vendor (and often rival channels) see who buys what at what price. No independently-provable record.
Naïve on-chain listing A tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record. Publishes prices, parties and terms to the world forever. Confidentiality is destroyed to gain the shared truth.
Point-to-point / off-platform deals Privacy by obscurity. No shared availability, so double-booking and circumvention are endemic; no neutral record when a dispute arises.

VRX's position

VRX takes the tamper-evident record from the on-chain approach and the confidentiality from the off-platform approach and reconciles them with a split: the chain carries proof-of-state over opaque keys; a private, access-controlled off-chain layer carries the meaning. The moat is the discipline of what is never written to the ledger at all, plus a vetted operator network layered on top.

Architecture · the one figure that matters

The on-chain / off-chain split

One rule governs the whole system: meaning never touches the chain. The figure shows what lives where, and the one-way binding between them.

Why one-way matters

An anchored seal_hash lets anyone later prove a specific off-chain record is the one that was committed (re-seal it, compare). It never lets anyone recover the record from the hash. Tamper-evidence without disclosure is the whole trick.

Architecture

One booking, end to end

Follow one booking from live availability to a confirmed hold, watching where meaning stays and only a hash moves.

The step sequence

  1. Operator stamp_tile → unit open (opaque unit_key).
  2. Agent open_claim (or burn_pass_hold on the booking-token path) → an opaque request_hash anchors the off-chain request; the unit moves to hold with a timed expiry.
  3. Off-chain: deposit recorded, buyer profile sealed, masked to the developer by default.
  4. Developer reply_claim / confirm_claim → state advances; a consented reveal of the buyer is a separate ceremony.
  5. On completion finalize_tile → done; expired holds auto-release back to open.

Privacy

The privacy model

Two distinct protections, applied per surface: on-chain opacity by construction, and off-chain access control by the server.

Protection 1 — on-chain opacity (by construction)

Everything on the contract is an opaque SHA-256 hash (unit_key, request_hash, seal_hash, commission_hash) plus neutral account ids and status codes. A chain scraper sees "account X holds an exclusive on hash 07f2…" — state, never meaning. The building, unit, price, party and terms cannot be derived from the chain. This moat holds by design.

Protection 2 — off-chain access control (by the server)

The plaintext that carries meaning lives off-chain, protected by server-side authorization + field-level masking. The engineering question for every counterparty-facing surface is: is this read scoped to a caller entitled to it, and masked server-side before the wire? A UI blur is theatre; masking happens on the server, so the plaintext never reaches the browser for a masked field.

Supporting mechanisms

  • Salted seals — tamper-evidence without reversibility; a per-record salt plus an optional global pepper held separately, so a breach that leaks the store alone cannot re-derive seals.
  • Encryption-at-rest for identity-document images — per-file key under an offline-sealed master key.
  • Append-only, hash-chained chat — the dispute trail is tamper-evident; editing or reordering a past message is detectable.
  • Provenance-stamped imagery — a leaked sensitive image is traceable back to the recipient it was served to; the stamping mechanism is deliberately private (the moat), and it is applied under a two-stage reveal (public exterior always; sensitive imagery only once a buyer is offer-eligible and consented).

The honest limit

On-chain opacity is absolute by construction. The off-chain layer's confidentiality rests on server-side authorization, not cryptography — so VRX must be trusted to run it correctly, and a standing security/privacy audit exists to make that trust checkable. That is where almost all the engineering attention goes.

The network

Roles & the vetted network

VRX is not only a contract — it is the vetting entity and the network operator. Identities enter the system only through VRX; the chain then anchors the fact of a sign-off, never its contents.

Eight participant roles: Developer (owns inventory, grants access, confirms bookings, may sell direct), Direct-seller (the developer's own desk), Agent and Agency, Buyer (a state, not a vetted role — any wallet), Direct-buyer (a walk-in meeting a light anti-abuse bar planned), CEO / Approver (an approval trust-dial), and Authority Review (sees vetted state for oversight, never private data). Onboarding a node or org is owner-gated; KYC is a human-led off-chain precondition, and nothing identifying ever lands on-chain.

Non-circumvention & booking tokens

Trust is enforced by visibility, not disclosure: the developer and agency see who holds a booking, not necessarily the private buyer, until a consented sealed reveal. Reservation capacity is itself a scarce, tamper-evident booking token that cascades down the trust chain (developer → agency → agent) and returns up on release — shown as "coins" in the wallet, backed on-chain by a non-transferable pass with burn + rebirth.

Contract maturity

The contract (Rust, near-sdk 5.26.1, #[near] macros) has grown through seven state schema versions, each added by an owner-only, borsh-safe migration that never touches an existing record. State changes emit structured NEP-297 events carrying only data already on-chain — opaque ids, account ids, status, version — never PII, amounts or terms.

Path: testnet (calculating-hand.testnet) → mainnet. No known privacy/correctness leak is open; the deferred items are deliberate and sequenced behind a dependency (for example, the authentication seam is built and default-off because the demo is deliberately wallet-less, with authorization already enforced server-side).

Legal posture (structural)

  • A facilitator and a sealer. VRX provides the private shared truth and the tamper-evident record. It records that a reservation, agreement or change happened, and seals it.
  • Not an adjudicator. VRX does not decide disputes. It records the evidence (before/after, who was notified, the sealed declarations) — never the dispute's resolution.
  • Not a warrantor of enforceability. VRX does not warrant that any underlying contract is legally enforceable. Parties' own terms and local law govern.
  • Privacy-by-design. The architecture itself (opaque on-chain, masked off-chain) is the primary privacy control, not a policy promise layered on afterwards.
