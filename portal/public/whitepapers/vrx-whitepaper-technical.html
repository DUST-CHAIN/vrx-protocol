Abstract

In one page

VRX is a reservation primitive: a callable NEAR smart contract that records who reserved what, paired with an off-chain portal that carries everything the chain must not — identities, prices, specifications and deal terms.

An operator with unique, non-substitutable inventory (unit 312 is not unit 311) needs to feed live availability to many sales teams and agencies who do not trust one another, and let them browse, book, reserve, take a deposit and hold — without double-booking, without leaking the commercial or personal details of a deal, and without a channel being able to go around the operator. A public chain gives tamper-evidence and independent verifiability but, used naïvely, publishes the very terms that make a deal confidential. A single vendor database gives confidentiality but centralizes trust and sees everything.

VRX's design answer is a strict split. The chain holds only opaque hashes and neutral account ids: it proves that a reservation exists and who holds it, and nothing else. The meaning — the what, the how-much, the with-whom — lives off-chain under server-side authorization and field-level masking. Privacy is therefore not a feature bolted on; it is a property of what is and is not written to the ledger.

Why it is domain-agnostic

Real estate (off-plan property) is the beachhead and the working proof. But the contract never "knows" it is real estate — every real-world reference is an opaque SHA-256 hash — so the same primitive serves cars, livestock, equipment, event slots: any Class-A, non-substitutable inventory. The contract models availability and holds over keys it cannot read.

Section 1

The problem

Selling scarce, one-of-a-kind inventory through many channels that do not trust each other breaks the tools everyone already uses. Three failures compound.

1 — Double-booking a unique thing

When inventory is fungible (seats, generic SKUs) an oversell is an inconvenience. When each item is unique — a specific apartment, a specific pedigree animal, a specific machine — two channels reserving "312" at once is a hard conflict that damages trust and, often, a contract. The market needs one authoritative answer to "is this exact unit still available?" that every channel reads the same way, in real time.

2 — Privacy leakage

The naïve fixes leak. A shared spreadsheet or a single vendor database exposes who is buying what, at what price, through whom — to the vendor and often to rival channels. Putting the registry on a public blockchain is worse: now prices, parties and terms are world-readable forever. The commercially-sensitive and personal details of a deal are exactly what must not be shared to get the shared truth.

3 — Circumvention / disintermediation

A channel that learns the end-buyer and the operator can be tempted to cut the introducer out and deal direct. The counter-measure — hiding the buyer's identity until a deal is firm — is itself a privacy problem, and it must be reconciled with the operator's legitimate need to confirm a booking.

The tension in one line

Everyone needs to agree on the same reservation state, while almost no one is entitled to see the same reservation details. A single store that satisfies the first requirement usually violates the second. VRX exists to hold both at once — and the whole architecture is a consequence of that single constraint.

Who feels it first

Off-plan real estate is the sharpest case and VRX's beachhead: a developer releases limited units to multiple agencies, each with multiple agents, selling to buyers who must stay confidential — with deposits, commissions and completion terms attached. Every failure above is routine there. The same shape recurs anywhere inventory is scarce, identified, and sold through intermediaries — which is why the on-chain layer is written to know nothing about real estate at all.

Section 2

What VRX is

A callable NEAR smart contract — the reservation and hold registry — plus an off-chain portal: the role-scoped apps each participant uses. Together they are the reservation primitive.

The primitive, precisely

VRX records reservations over non-substitutable inventory. Its on-chain state is deliberately thin. A unit (a tile) is open, on hold, closed, or done — encoded as the small integers 0/1/2/3. A claim is open, accepted, declined, offered-an-alternative, or confirmed (0/1/2/3/4). An exclusive window is held by one agency or it is not. Everything that would identify the unit, the buyer, the price or the terms is an opaque hash. The contract does not model "apartment" or "car" — it models availability and holds over keys it cannot read.

312 ≠ 311

Value in this system scales with the worth of the specific reserved item. The primitive's whole reason to exist is that substituting one unit for another is not acceptable — so the registry tracks each unit individually, keyed by an opaque unit_key, and a hold on one is never a hold on another.

Section 2.5

Where it sits

Why nothing already in the market does this. Stated plainly — the moat is a design choice others have not made, not a claim of being uniquely clever.

Three existing approaches, and where each leaks

ApproachGives youWhat it costs
Generic booking / CRM software A shared calendar and pipeline. Centralizes trust in one vendor's database; the vendor (and often rival channels) can see who buys what at what price. No independently-provable record; the operator still owns the single point of truth and failure.
Naïve on-chain listing A tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record. Publishes prices, parties and terms to the world forever. The confidentiality that makes a deal a deal is destroyed to gain the shared truth.
Point-to-point / off-platform deals Privacy by obscurity. No shared availability, so double-booking and circumvention are endemic; no neutral record when a dispute arises.

VRX's position

VRX takes the tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record from the on-chain approach and the confidentiality from the off-platform approach, and reconciles them with a split: the chain carries proof-of-state over opaque keys; a private, access-controlled off-chain layer carries the meaning. The moat is not a faster database — it is the discipline of what is never written to the ledger at all, plus a vetted operator network layered on top.

Honest limits of the claim

  • The off-chain layer's confidentiality rests on server-side authorization, not cryptography — VRX must be trusted to run it correctly. The audit (§5) is how that trust is made checkable.
  • On-chain opacity is absolute by construction, but it only protects what lives on-chain; a leak, if it happened, would come from the off-chain layer — which is where almost all the engineering attention goes.
  • VRX is not the only system splitting on/off chain. Its specific contribution is the reservation-primitive framing, the vetted network, and a provenance/forensic layer — not the generic idea of anchoring a hash.

Section 3 · Architecture

The on-chain / off-chain split

One rule governs the whole system: meaning never touches the chain. The figure shows what lives where, and the one-way binding between them.

Why one-way matters

An anchored seal_hash lets anyone later prove a specific off-chain record is the one that was committed (re-seal it, compare). It never lets anyone recover the record from the hash. Tamper-evidence without disclosure is the whole trick — and it is the property every design decision downstream protects.

The borsh-safe migration pattern

Borsh is order-based and ignores field names, so two rules keep migrations safe. First: never append a field to a struct that is stored as a value — that would shift the byte layout and break decode on every old entry. Instead, extra per-key data lives in a sidecar map (that is what pass_links, hold_expiry and exclusives are). Second: each migration reads the prior versioned struct, carries every existing map over unchanged, and only creates the one new top-level map empty. Each is owner-gated — the caller must equal the owner recorded in the pre-migration state — and emits a Migrate event.

// migrate7 (V6 -> V7): add the empty `attestations` map; carry everything else over.
// Owner-only, pure-additive, borsh-safe — no existing record is touched.
#[init(ignore_state)]
pub fn migrate7() -> Self {
    let v6: ContractV6 = env::state_read().expect(err::NO_V6_STATE);
    assert_eq!(env::predecessor_account_id(), v6.owner, "{}", err::NOT_OWNER);
    Event::Migrate { from: 6, to: 7 }.emit();
    Self {
        owner: v6.owner, nodes: v6.nodes, orgs: v6.orgs, peers: v6.peers,
        gates: v6.gates, tiles: v6.tiles, claims: v6.claims, queue: v6.queue,
        /* ...passes, pass_links, hold_expiry, agreements, toll, treasury, exempt, exclusives... */
        attestations: UnorderedMap::new(SK::At),  // the only new map, created empty
    }
}

Field-name discipline

Source field names are readable; each pins its original compact JSON key via #[serde(rename)], so the view-API wire shape is frozen while the code stays legible. Because borsh ignores names, storage layout is byte-identical across the rename — but never reorder a field, since that would change the byte layout.

Section 3 · Architecture

Data flow, end to end

Follow one booking from live availability to a confirmed hold, watching where meaning stays and only a hash moves.

The step sequence

  1. Operator stamp_tile(unit_key, 0, integrity_hash) → the unit is open.
  2. Agent open_claim (or burn_pass_hold on the booking-token path) → an opaque request_hash anchors the off-chain request; the unit moves to hold with a timed expiry in hold_expiry.
  3. Off-chain: deposit recorded, buyer profile sealed, masked to the developer by default.
  4. Developer reply_claim / confirm_claim → claim status advances; a consented reveal of the buyer is a separate ceremony.
  5. On completion finalize_tile → done; an expired hold auto-releases back to open (via release / expiry).

Section 4 · Privacy

The privacy model

Two distinct protections, applied per surface: on-chain opacity by construction, and off-chain access control by the server. This section is the public, capability-and-design level of the model.

Protection 1 — on-chain opacity (by construction)

Everything on the contract is an opaque SHA-256 hash (unit_key, request_hash, seal_hash, commission_hash) plus neutral account ids and status integers. A chain scraper sees "account X holds an exclusive on hash 07f2…" — state, never meaning. The building, unit, price, party and terms cannot be derived from the chain. This moat holds by design and needs no further work.

The seal construction (public part)

A seal is a salted SHA-256 over an off-chain record. The salt is per-record; an optional global pepper is held separately from the record store, so a breach that leaks the store alone cannot re-derive seals. A seal is a commitment, not an encryption key — it proves a specific record is the one that was committed (re-seal and compare) and reveals nothing about the record's contents. Where a real-world identifier must be a stable opaque key (so two references to the same thing collide deterministically), the key is an HMAC under a server-held key rather than a bare hash — same opacity, with unforgeability against an outsider who does not hold the key.

Protection 2 — off-chain access control (by the server)

The plaintext that carries meaning lives off-chain, protected by server-side authorization + field-level masking, not by cryptography. The engineering question for every counterparty-facing surface is therefore: is this read scoped to a caller entitled to it, and masked server-side before the wire? A UI blur is theatre; masking happens on the server, so the plaintext never reaches the browser for a masked field.

Supporting mechanisms (built)

  • Salted seals (lib/seal.ts) — tamper-evidence without reversibility, as above.
  • Encryption-at-rest for identity-document images — a per-file data-encryption key under an offline-sealed key-encryption key.
  • Append-only, hash-chained chat (lib/channels.ts) — each message folds the prior record's hash into its own, so editing or reordering a past message breaks the chain and is detectable.
  • Provenance-stamped imagery — a leaked sensitive image is traceable back to the recipient it was served to. The stamping mechanism is deliberately private and is not described in this paper.

The seven invariants an auditor should test

  1. No PII or plaintext terms on-chain — only opaque hashes + neutral ids.
  2. Every counterparty-facing buyer projection is masked server-side — the plaintext never reaches the browser for a masked field.
  3. Every reveal is a witnessed, sealed, consented act — never a silent flip; the sealed declaration is on the trail even on decline.
  4. The chat is append-only + hash-chained — editing / reordering / deleting a past message is detectable and prevented.
  5. A seal re-verifies iff the plaintext is unaltered — tamper or a lost salt returns false, never a silent pass.
  6. Self-read full, counterparty-read masked — on every role route.
  7. No silent on-contract change — every change to a partnered building seals a before/after evidence record and notifies affected parties; the most sensitive particulars change only via a VRX-reviewed correction.

The one deliberate inversion (an auditor must understand this)

The system masks a buyer to a counterparty by default everywhere. Exactly one place inverts: the prior-block force-show. When a desk hard-blocks a buyer, that buyer's full identity is thereafter force-shown to that desk only — so a blocked bad actor cannot re-approach the same desk anonymously. It is desk-scoped (never global), triggered only by a deliberate block, timestamped, and applied server-side in that desk's projection alone. An intentional, scoped, logged anti-cheat exception — not a leak.

Section 6

Vetting & the operator network

VRX is not only a contract — it is the vetting entity and the network operator. Identities enter the system only through VRX; the chain then anchors the fact of a sign-off.

Owner-gated onboarding

Provisioning a node (seller) or org (agency) is owner-only: the contract owner is the vetting identity. KYC / vetting is a human-led off-chain precondition done before the on-chain register_node / register_org call — nothing identifying ever lands on-chain. A developer's captive in-house sales team is simply an org provisioned the same way; there is no special path. The same owner-gating protects the admin surface (set_toll, set_treasury, the exempt list) and every migration.

Section 6

Non-circumvention & booking tokens

Two mechanisms keep the network honest: the buyer stays masked until a deal is firm (so a channel cannot be cut out), and reservation capacity is a scarce, tamper-evident token that cascades down the trust chain.

Non-circumvention

Trust is enforced by visibility, not disclosure. An agent holding a booking has the unit off-market; the developer and agency see who holds it but not necessarily the private buyer behind it until a consented, sealed reveal (§4). A non-circumvention declaration is sealed as evidence at the reveal request — even if the reveal is later declined — so the introduction is on the tamper-evident trail.

The booking-token cascade

VRX issues a developer a scarce quantity of booking tokens — reservation capacity. These are handed or sold down the trust chain (developer → agency → agent), with a tamper-evident receipt at each hand-off. An agent holding a token can secure a booking and take a deposit. Capacity is scarce and reusable: a released or cancelled token returns up the chain. Tokens surface as "coins" in the wallet.

On-chain support

The contract backs this with a non-transferable Pass (mint_passes, owner → agent), burn_pass_hold (spend a pass to open an instant hold), and rebirth_pass (a burned pass returns as a fresh one, its lineage recorded in the pass_links sidecar). The exclusive-hold primitive (grant_exclusive) grants one agency the sole right to book a unit for a bounded window, auto-reverting to the shared pool at expiry (release_exclusive_if_expired).

Booking tokens — reservation capacity

Distinct from $VRX: a booking token is a unit of capacity to reserve, not a credit. It is scarce, cascades down the trust chain, and returns on release (§6). On-chain it is the non-transferable Pass with burn + rebirth.

Commissions & settlement projection

A commission Agreement is keyed "{node}:{org}" — the same key as the access gate, so an org↔node relationship has two parallel on-chain records: the gate (access scope) and the agreement (commission). The on-chain record holds only an opaque HMAC anchor (commission_hash) of the off-chain commission rulebook plus a version; the actual percentages, tiers, splits and amounts never appear on-chain. Money compute and owed→paid tracking stay off-chain (anchor-only). On-rail settlement is planned; VRX today records payments as a two-sided attestation ledger and does not move money in-app.

How values are handled (stated plainly)

  • Prices, plans, splits and amounts are computed and stored off-chain only; the chain carries a versioned hash anchor, never a number.
  • A commission rulebook is versioned: changing it writes a new version + a new anchor, so a settlement projection always references a specific sealed rulebook version rather than a mutable "current" value.
  • Rounding and currency handling are an off-chain concern (tested under lib/price-rounding.ts and the settlement-projection suite); the contract never performs money arithmetic.
  • The protocol usage toll (toll_yocto) is the only value the contract itself moves — a flat, owner-set amount in yoctoNEAR forwarded to the treasury, waived for exempt callers. It is unrelated to deal terms.

Section 8

Legal posture

The posture is structural and deliberately narrow: VRX facilitates and seals; it does not adjudicate and does not warrant enforceability.

What VRX is, legally

  • A facilitator and a sealer. VRX provides the private shared truth and the tamper-evident record. It records that a reservation, agreement or change happened, and seals it.
  • Not an adjudicator. VRX does not decide disputes and does not determine the outcome of a disagreement between parties. It records the evidence (before/after, who was notified, the sealed declarations) — never the dispute's resolution.
  • Not a warrantor of enforceability. VRX does not warrant that any underlying contract is legally enforceable. Parties' own terms and local law govern.
  • Privacy-by-design. The architecture itself (opaque on-chain, masked off-chain) is the primary privacy control, not a policy promise layered on afterwards.

Why the narrowness is a feature

By refusing to adjudicate or warrant, VRX stays a neutral infrastructure provider across jurisdictions and asset classes. The sealed, append-only record it produces is designed to be useful evidence to the parties and their own counsel — not a substitute for them. This is what keeps the primitive domain-agnostic and multi-jurisdiction-ready (multi-jurisdiction packs are planned).

The regulator / oversight view

The Authority-Review role is the legal posture made concrete: an oversight party can confirm the vetted state of the system without ever seeing private buyer data. Oversight of process, not exposure of parties. This is the same opacity invariant from §4 extended to the regulator surface — the moat does not have a privileged bypass.

The contract ships a reproducible end-to-end walk runnable with npm run demo (from the portal) — a near-workspaces sandbox that stamps a tile, opens and confirms a claim, and exercises holds against a real in-process NEAR runtime. The live portal demo is at vrx.poolo.org — a seeded sandbox: enter wallet-free via ?enter=1, assume any role (Developer / Agency / Agent / Buyer) from the top bar, and walk one deal end to end. Write actions in the sandbox are simulated; the contract on NEAR testnet is the honesty anchor, not the sandbox clicks.

One paragraph, for a protocol reader

VRX turns a public chain's strength — a tamper-evident, independently-verifiable record — into something usable for confidential commerce by writing only opaque proof-of-state on-chain and keeping every meaningful detail in a vetted, access-controlled off-chain layer. The contract is a thin, domain-agnostic state machine over hashes; the portal is where authorization, masking and sealing live. The split is the product.
